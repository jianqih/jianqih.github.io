Working Papers

  1. Artificial Intelligence and College Major Choice
    (with Kelvin Yuen)
    July 2026
    Abstract
    This paper studies how the diffusion of artificial intelligence reshapes human capital investment and local labor market outcomes. Using Lightcast job postings and American Community Survey data, we construct location-level AI labor demand shocks and identify their effects with a shift-share IV design. Higher AI demand reallocates college majors toward AI-adjacent fields—especially computer science and engineering—and away from education and humanities. Dynamic estimates show persistent relative gains in these AI-adjacent STEM fields and relative declines in humanities, business and economics after 4 years. On the extensive margin, AI demand has limited effects on aggregate college enrollment and the college wage premium. AI demand also raises local income on average, with gains concentrated in initially richer locations, leading to spatial disparity.
  2. Fixed Masses, Moving Locations: A Quantile Method for Heterogeneous-Agent Models
    August 2026
    Abstract
    This paper develops a Lagrangian representation for heterogeneous-agent models: fixed probability masses carried at moving quantile locations. An exact closed-form recompression restores the quantile cells after each policy update while preserving their first moment. In the Krusell–Smith economy, the method approximates the aggregate-saving moment behind factor prices 13 to 69 times more accurately than a matched-dimension uniform lottery histogram over 8 to 128 cells per earnings type; a hand-tuned quadratic fixed grid narrows the residual advantage to 1.3 to 3.3. At a 1% TFP shock, the nonlinear transition matches a Sequence-Space Jacobian benchmark; at 5% and 10% shocks, it tracks the fine-grid nonlinear transition 14 to 40 times more closely than the histogram. In a large-transfer shock, it also yields substantially smaller aggregate-consumption-path errors than matched histogram lotteries.
